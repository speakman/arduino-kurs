TASK: Create a high-end editorial 3D render of a standard half-size
solderless breadboard, presented in the style of a Wired-magazine
"how it works" illustration or an Apple-style product render. This is
NOT a photograph — it is a clean, polished, studio-quality 3D
illustration where the upper plastic body is rendered as a
semi-transparent translucent shell, and the internal phosphor-bronze
metal contact strips are clearly the visual focal point.

CAMERA & PERSPECTIVE:
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

3/4 perspective view — the camera is positioned approximately 25 degrees
tilted forward from straight-down (so you see the top of the board
clearly but also catch a hint of the front edge and side edge giving
depth). Slight rotation around the vertical axis (about 10 degrees) so
one side of the board is slightly closer than the other, adding subtle
isometric-like depth. The viewing angle should feel like an editorial
3D illustration — clearly three-dimensional but not so steep that the
row numbers and column letters become illegible.

The entire breadboard fits in the frame with generous margins. Portrait
orientation overall. The board appears to "float" slightly above the
background surface with a soft contact shadow underneath.

PLASTIC BODY (TRANSPARENT SHELL):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The breadboard's outer plastic body is rendered as a TRANSLUCENT shell —
approximately 25-30% opacity, glass-frosted-acrylic appearance. The
plastic is white-tinted (#f8f8f5) but mostly see-through, like very
lightly frosted glass or pearlescent acrylic. You can clearly see the

Render the plastic with subtle thickness (~9mm) so you can see it has
volume — particularly visible at the front and side edges due to the
3/4 perspective angle.

The 400+ holes are visible as crisp rectangular cutouts in the plastic
top surface, each leading down to the metal contact strip beneath.

PRESERVE THESE PRINTED MARKINGS on the plastic top surface (rendered
opaque, on top of the translucent plastic, faithful to a real
breadboard):
- All 60 row numbers (1 through 30, printed in dark gray on both the
  left side near columns a-e AND the right side near columns f-j)
- Column letters at TOP and BOTTOM of the terminal area, exact

- "+" and "−" symbols at both ends of each of the four power rails
- Red printed line/stripe along each "+" rail
- Blue printed line/stripe along each "−" rail
- The vertical center groove between columns e and f

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
METAL CONTACT STRIPS (THE VISUAL FOCAL POINT):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The phosphor-bronze contact strips inside the breadboard are the HERO
of this image. They should be clearly visible, prominently rendered,
with full 3D form (not flat silhouettes).

MATERIAL: Polished phosphor-bronze (warm golden-brown, #c08850 to
#8b6940 gradient), with realistic metallic shading — subtle specular
highlights catching the studio key light, soft fill shadows on the
opposite side, hint of warm rim-light along edges. The metal looks
real, freshly stamped, slightly oxidized but still gleaming.

LAYOUT (electrically critical — must be correct):

1. TERMINAL STRIPS (in the central 5-column area on each side of the
   center groove):
   - For EACH of the 30 numbered rows, TWO separate short horizontal
     metal strips:
       (a) one running under columns a-b-c-d-e (covers 5 holes)
       (b) one running under columns f-g-h-i-j (covers 5 holes)
   - Each strip is approximately 12mm long × 3mm wide × 0.4mm thick.
   - Each strip has 5 visible "spring fingers" — small upward-curving
     metal tabs, one under each hole, that grip an inserted component
     leg. These spring fingers should be visible as small 3D bumps or
     curved tabs along the top surface of each strip.
   - The strip for row N (a-e) is COMPLETELY SEPARATE from the strip
     for row N (f-j) — clear physical gap between them aligned with
     the center groove.
   - The strip for row N is COMPLETELY SEPARATE from the strip for row
     N+1 — small visible gap (~1mm) of nothing between adjacent rows.
   - Total: 60 terminal strips visible (30 rows × 2 strips per row).

2. POWER RAIL STRIPS (along the four rail edges):
   - Along EACH of the four power rails (left +, left −, right +,
     right −): ONE long single continuous metal strip running the FULL
     length of the board. Approximately 70mm long × 3mm wide × 0.4mm
     thick, with 30 evenly-spaced spring fingers along its length
     (one under each rail hole).
   - Total: 4 long power rail strips.
   - The left "+" rail strip is NOT connected to the right "+" rail
     strip (no metal bridge between them).
   - The "+" and "−" strips on the same side are also separate from
     each other — visible gap between them.

3. ALL strips have realistic dimensional 3D form — viewer should clearly
   perceive their thickness, the curve of the spring fingers, and the
   metallic specular response.
